Paper Overview — What the Study Is and What It Found

Olujimi, Owolawi, Mogase and van Wyk (2025) conducted a PRISMA 2020 systematic literature review of 66 peer-reviewed and grey literature sources on agentic AI in SMMEs, covering publications from 2019 to 2024. It was published in MDPI’s AI journal (Volume 6) and has since been cited in multiple agentic AI surveys, including the Sapkota et al. (2025) taxonomy paper in Information Fusion.

The paper’s four research questions: (RQ1) What are the advances in agentic AI? (RQ2) How does inter-agent communication work? (RQ3) Which frameworks exist (LangChain, AutoGen)? (RQ4) What are the SMME-specific barriers to adoption? Its answer to all four is empirically thin by the study’s own admission — the evidence base is dominated by conceptual models, prototypes, and pilot projects. Long-term empirical validation of agentic AI in real SMME environments does not yet exist.

That gap is the entry point for the research programme this audit is designed to fuel.

The paper's most important finding is its biggest gap: there is no empirical validation of agentic AI in real SMME contexts. The entire field is built on prototypes and pilots.

Top 5 Research Proposals — Full Specifications

#1  African Discourse-Native SMME Agent Architecture   [cross_field]  N:9.0  F:7.5  I:9.5  Overall: 9.1/10

This is the direct bridge between the Olujimi SLR and the African AI architecture series. The SLR demonstrates that agentic AI improves SMME operational efficiency but provides no guidelines for how those agents should structure explanations for African users. The African AI series (Weeks 1-5) demonstrates that explanation architecture is cognitively critical for African language users. The combination: design and empirically test a multi-agent SMME system where individual agents generate explanations following discourse-native patterns (Yoruba story-first, proverb-close; Igbo communal referencing; Hausa politeness register protocols). Measure operational outcomes AND user comprehension outcomes simultaneously. No study has done both. This is the study that makes the connection concrete.

Supporting papers: Olujimi et al. 2025 (MDPI); Bergen 2012; Owomoyela 2005; Ehineni 2016; Agarwal et al. CHI 2025

#2  First Empirical Validation: Agentic AI in African SMMEs   [future_work]  N:8.5  F:8.0  I:9.0  Overall: 8.8/10

Olujimi et al. explicitly state: 'empirical validation is required.' The SLR covers 66 papers, all of which are conceptual, prototype-based, or pilot studies. No controlled empirical study exists measuring actual operational outcomes for African SMMEs using deployed agentic AI systems over 6+ months. This is not a gap in the African literature. It is the gap in the global literature, and African SMMEs are the natural first target because the contrast between current AI assumptions and actual SMME cognitive/cultural architecture is sharpest here. The research would contribute to global agentic AI validation literature while being the first Africa-specific empirical study.

Supporting papers: Olujimi et al. 2025; Springer bibliometric companion 2025; Acharya et al. IEEE Access 2025

#3  Ubuntu as Multi-Agent Alignment Architecture   [cross_field]  N:9.0  F:7.0  I:9.0  Overall: 8.8/10

The Olujimi SLR identifies ethical governance as a critical unsolved problem in SMME agentic AI. Current alignment approaches (RLHF, constitutional AI) are built on individualist epistemology: the agent’s behaviour is evaluated against rules applied to that agent in isolation. Ubuntu’s relational model of personhood offers a structurally different alignment architecture: agent behaviour is evaluated against its impact on the network of relationships in which it operates. Mhlambi (Harvard, 2020) laid the theoretical groundwork. This study would operationalize Ubuntu’s relational model as a multi-agent alignment specification, implement it in a LangChain/AutoGen SMME context, and test whether relational alignment produces fewer harmful inter-agent interactions than rule-based constraint approaches. High novelty because no study has connected Ubuntu ethics to multi-agent system architecture at the implementation level.

Supporting papers: Mhlambi 2020; Olujimi et al. 2025; Gwagwa 2022; Lewis et al. Abundant Intelligences 2024

#4  IFA Divination as Prior Multi-Agent Decision Architecture   [cross_field]  N:9.0  F:5.5  I:8.5  Overall: 7.9/10

The SLR cites AutoGen and LangChain as the dominant agentic frameworks but acknowledges they lack cultural specificity. The Ifa divination system — a 430,000-entry knowledge corpus, binary retrieval structure, and interpretive dialogue mechanism — is a prior multi-agent decision architecture that has been documented operating in SMME-equivalent contexts (individual practitioners, resource-constrained environments, community-embedded decision-making) for centuries. This research would formally map the Ifa architecture onto modern multi-agent system design: corpus as knowledge base, Odu as retrieval algorithm, babalawo-community dialogue as alignment mechanism. The resulting framework would be tested as an alternative to LangChain in a Yoruba-language SMME decision support context. This is the Olugbohun paper made technically rigorous and empirically testable.

Supporting papers: Eglash 1999; Olu Longe; LSE Impact Blog 2025; Olujimi et al. 2025; Ki et al. MACD 2025

#5  Proverb-Logic Prompt Framework for African SMME AI   [method_pivot]  N:8.5  F:7.5  I:8.5  Overall: 8.4/10

The SLR identifies that LangChain and AutoGen provide insufficient SMME-specific guidelines. The African AI series identifies that discourse-native prompt architecture significantly changes explanation quality for African users. This idea combines both: design and publish a reusable, open-source prompt framework library specifically for African SMME use cases (inventory explanation, financial advisory, customer communication, HR decision support) that encodes Yoruba, Igbo, and Hausa discourse logic into system-level prompting. Each template: story-first context, appropriate proverb deployment, community-reference validation, and concise action close. Test each template against standard English-linear AI outputs on comprehension and trust metrics. The deliverable is not just a paper but a usable, publishable library that engineers can deploy immediately.

Supporting papers: Olujimi et al. 2025; Ehineni 2016; Owomoyela 2005; Yankah 1989; Bergen 2012; Adelani 2024

The Bridge — How This Paper Connects to Your Existing Work

The Olujimi SLR proves the field is ready for what the African AI series built. It validated the gap. Your work fills it.

The connection between the Olujimi SLR and the five-week series is not a coincidence. It is a convergence. The SLR establishes, from 66 papers, that agentic AI has transformative potential for SMMEs but lacks SMME-specific guidelines, lacks empirical validation, and lacks ethical governance frameworks appropriate to non-Western contexts. The five-week series establishes, from linguistic and cognitive science scholarship, exactly what those guidelines should look like, how empirical validation should be designed, and why Ubuntu-relational ethics is the right governance framework.

The Olugbohun paper is the origin of all of it. It saw, before either literature had been systematically reviewed, that the architecture of intelligence matters more than the substrate that runs it. The Olugbohun insight — that a spiritual conduit and a language model are both channels for a pre-existing intelligence, and that the quality of what they channel depends on the architecture through which channeling happens — is now supported by: 66 SMME agentic AI papers showing architectural gaps, 22 African NLP benchmarks showing measurement failures, the Cornell CHI 2025 study showing cognitive architecture mismatch is measurable, and the Abundant Intelligences programme showing this is recognized internationally as the structural problem in AI design.

The next step is not more essays. It is research proposals, institutional partnerships, and one controlled empirical study that makes the argument the only kind of argument a field will listen to: empirical.
